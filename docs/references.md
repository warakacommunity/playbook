# References

Works cited across the AfriPlaybook, in one place. Every entry has a stable anchor, so an in-text
citation links straight to its exact position here. To add a reference, give its heading the author
and year with an id (`### Surname, I., et al. (year) {#surname-year}`; a dataset or benchmark name such as
`{#afriqa-2023}` is also fine) and cite it from the text as `([Surname et al., year](../references.md#surname-year))`.
Entries are in alphabetical order by heading.


### Abbas, A., et al. (2023) {#abbas-2023}

Abbas, A., Tirumala, K., Simig, D., Ganguli, S., & Morcos, A. S. *SemDeDup: Data-efficient learning at web-scale through semantic deduplication.* arXiv:2303.09540. [arxiv.org/abs/2303.09540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09540)

### Abdulmumin, I., et al. (2026) {#abdulmumin-2026}

*Temporal Simultaneity Predicts Annotation Quality in Sentiment Corpora.* arXiv:2605.27239. A Setswana
sentiment dataset (3,565 tweets, three native-speaker annotators across eight batches): overall agreement
was κ = 0.76, but items labelled within a minute of each other reached κ = 0.98 while items labelled more
than a day apart fell to κ = 0.65, revealing annotator drift over time. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.27239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27239)

### Access Now (2026) {#accessnow-2026}

Access Now. *Resilience and resistance: internet shutdowns in Africa in 2025.* #KeepItOn press release, 31 March 2026. Reports that the internet was shut down 30 times in 15 African countries in 2025, with protests the leading trigger in the region. [accessnow.org](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/resilience-and-resistance-internet-shutdowns-in-africa-in-2025/)

### Adebara, I., et al. (2022) {#afrolid-2022}

Adebara, I., Elmadany, A., Abdul-Mageed, M., & Inciarte, A. *AfroLID: A Neural Language Identification Tool for African Languages.* Proceedings of EMNLP 2022, 1958–1981. Covers 517 African languages and varieties across 14 language families and five scripts. [aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.128](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.128/) (DOI: [10.18653/v1/2022.emnlp-main.128](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2022.emnlp-main.128))

### Adebara, I., et al. (2023) {#serengeti-2023}

*SERENGETI: Massively Multilingual Language Models for Africa.* Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2023, pp. 1498–1537. Pretrained language models covering 517 African languages and language varieties. [aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.97](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.97/)

### Adelani, D. I., et al. (2021) {#adelani-2021}

*MasakhaNER: Named Entity Recognition for African Languages.* Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics, 9, 1116–1131. The first large, publicly available NER dataset (PER, ORG, LOC, DATE) for ten African languages, annotated by volunteer native speakers from the Masakhane community. [aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.66](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.66/)

### Adelani, D. I., et al. (2022a) {#adelani-2022}

*MasakhaNER 2.0: Africa-centric Transfer Learning for Named Entity Recognition.* Proceedings of
EMNLP 2022. A human-annotated NER dataset spanning 20 African languages, built by the Masakhane
community. [aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.298](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.298/)

### Adelani, D. I., et al. (2022b) {#mafand-2022}

*A Few Thousand Translations Go a Long Way! Leveraging Pre-trained Models for African News Translation
(MAFAND-MT).* Proceedings of NAACL 2022. Human-translated news parallel data for ~16–21 African languages
from the Masakhane community. [aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.223](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.223/)

### Adelani, D. I., et al. (2024) {#irokobench-2024}

*IrokoBench: A New Benchmark for African Languages in the Age of Large Language Models.* NAACL 2025;
arXiv:2406.03368. Human-translated benchmark for 17 African languages: AfriXNLI (NLI), AfriMGSM (math
reasoning), and AfriMMLU (knowledge QA). [arxiv.org/abs/2406.03368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03368)

### African Next Voices (2025) {#african-next-voices}

*African Next Voices.* A Gates Foundation–funded initiative (about US\$2.2 million) that recorded roughly
9,000 hours of everyday speech in 18 African languages across Kenya, Nigeria, and South Africa, collected
by local centres such as the Maseno Centre for Applied AI and Data Science Nigeria. One of the largest
African voice datasets of its kind. [up.ac.za/afridsai](https://www.up.ac.za/afridsai/news/african-next-voices-featured-nature-reshaping-speech-technology-africa)

### AfriCaption (2025) {#africaption-2025}

Oduwole, M., Mireku, P., Adebanjo, F., Olajide, O., Aliyu, M. A., & Novikova, J. *AfriCaption: Establishing a New Paradigm for Image Captioning in African Languages.* arXiv:2510.17405. Flickr8k captions machine-translated (NLLB-200, M2M-100 and Azure Translate) into 20 African languages, including Hausa, Igbo, Yorùbá, Ewe, Luganda and Kinyarwanda, filtered by back-translation similarity, with native-speaker adequacy ratings for Yorùbá, Igbo, Hausa and Ewe and a 0.5B-parameter SigLIP + NLLB-200 captioning model. [arxiv.org/abs/2510.17405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17405)

### AfriDoc-MT (2025) {#afridocmt-2025}

*AFRIDOC-MT: Document-level MT Corpus for African Languages.* Masakhane, Lacuna-funded; EMNLP 2025;
arXiv:2501.06374. Document-level English to Amharic, Hausa, Swahili, Yorùbá, and Zulu (health + IT news);
finds that sentence-trained models generalise poorly to documents. [arxiv.org/abs/2501.06374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06374)

### AfriScience-MT (2026) {#afriscience-2026}

*AfriScience-MT: Towards Decolonizing Science in Africa through Text Translation.* arXiv:2605.29741. A
scientific/STEM parallel corpus for six African languages across 11 domains, with translators coining new
terminology where none existed. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.29741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29741)

[doi.org/10.1007/s44163-025-00227-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-025-00227-7)

### Agić, Ž., & Vulić, I. (2019) {#agic-2019}

[aclanthology.org/P19-1310](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1310/)

### Aguilar, G., Kar, S., & Solorio, T. (2020) {#aguilar-2020}

*LinCE: A Centralized Benchmark for Linguistic Code-switching Evaluation.* Proceedings of LREC 2020. [arxiv.org/abs/2005.04322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04322)

### Ahia, O., et al. (2023) {#ahia-2023}

Ahia, O., Kumar, S., Gonen, H., Kasai, J., Mortensen, D., Smith, N., & Tsvetkov, Y. *Do All Languages Cost the Same? Tokenization in the Era of Commercial Language Models.* Proceedings of EMNLP 2023, 9904–9923. [aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.614](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.614/)

### Ajami HTR (2025) {#ajami-htr-2025}

*A Handwritten Text Recognition Dataset for Ajami Manuscripts in Fulfulde and Hausa.* 2025. Manually

### Akyon, F. C., Altinuc, S. O., & Temizel, A. (2022) {#akyon-2022}

[doi.org/10.1109/ICIP46576.2022.9897990](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICIP46576.2022.9897990) (arXiv: [2202.06934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06934))

### Andriluka, M., et al. (2014) {#andriluka-2014}

### Anikina, T., et al. (2025) {#llm-datagen-2025}
